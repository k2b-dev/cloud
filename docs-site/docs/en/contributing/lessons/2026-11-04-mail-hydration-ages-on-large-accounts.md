### Message bodies waited a full round of folder syncs on a large account

- **What happened:** On a production account with 354 folders, body hydration waited about 957 s per turn on the provider lease. That delayed the content of newly arrived mail as well as repaired bodies.
- **Root cause:** Folder syncs ask for the lease with `sync` priority and a 60-second head start. Mailbox hydration asks with `background` priority and none, or with `sync` for a first arrival batch. The line orders waiters by place, and a round over 354 folders takes longer than 60 s. So the line held about one sync per folder, each with a better place than the body's, and the body waited until the round had passed. The head start is meant to bound how long later work may go first, but the length of the line, which grows with the folders, bounded nothing.
- **Error class:** A priority by head start bounds the wait only while the line is shorter than the head start; with a line that grows with the account, low-priority work waits proportionally to the account's size.
- **Guard:** A waiter that asks to age ranks as a command once it has waited in line for the head start that syncs have over background work: 60 s, derived from the existing head starts. The wait counts from the waiter's first ask, which the line keeps apart from its place, because the place moves when the priority it asks with changes. Mailbox hydration asks to age, so it alternates with folder syncs from then on. Its turn counts as a command turn, so the next turn goes to the other work. A batch still yields after one body while a sync waits, and its next batch lines up anew and must age again. The 2026-11-01 lesson stays intact: arrival bodies do not rank above folder continuations up front, and a backlog of bodies takes at most one turn per aging period while syncs wait. `provider-lease-fairness.integration.test.ts` holds the lease with 354 waiting folder syncs. Before aging, a folder sync goes first. After aging, the body takes the next turn and loads one body, and the next folder sync follows. The test fails without aging. A second test checks that a change of priority while waiting neither resets nor shortens the wait.
