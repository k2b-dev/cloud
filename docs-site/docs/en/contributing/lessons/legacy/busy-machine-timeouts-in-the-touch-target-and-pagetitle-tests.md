### Busy-machine timeouts in the touch-target and PageTitle tests

- **What happened:** In WebKit on a busy machine, the touch-target case "keep the message field's edge above the chat composer footer, …" and the PageTitle case "an app's own title takes the same height at every depth, …" hit bun's 5000 ms test timeout, PageTitle in 3 of 5 runs ([#1497](https://github.com/k2b-dev/cloud/issues/1497)). In Chromium, the touch-target case "give stacked DetailPanel.Action rows a 44 px row …" timed out once in 20 runs ([#1485](https://github.com/k2b-dev/cloud/issues/1485)). The values that the tests check were never wrong.
- **Root cause:** The fixed work of the tests, not the product. Each step was measured with `performance.now()` in five parallel runs at a load of 44 to 53 on 24 cores. The DetailPanel case tapped every pixel of every control twice: 129,440 `elementFromPoint` calls, 3.5 s median in Chromium and 2.6 s in WebKit. The composer case opened five pages (1.6 s in WebKit), loaded eight documents that each parsed the inlined 857 KB `dist/styles.css` again (1.4 s), and scanned for 1.4 s. The PageTitle case opened six pages (1.8 s in WebKit) and loaded six documents with six fixture bundles (1.8 s). In 10 such runs per engine, the composer case timed out 6 times in WebKit and the DetailPanel case 4 times in Chromium. Styling and laying out the composer itself took less than 10 ms per state.
- **Error class:** Test work sized for an idle machine.
- **Guard:** Each test opens one page and changes its states inside it; the checks for a device without a touch screen are tests of their own. `show()` in `touch-targets.browser.test.ts` parses the stylesheet once per page and replaces the body for each state. States with toasts load a new document each, because the toast module keeps its toasts, and the reach check reads the document of the scan. The PageTitle fixture changes the depth through `setOwnDepth`, and both locales load into one page. The pixel scan taps only the pixels within 2 px of another control's hit area, which it reads from the used insets of each `::after`. It fails when switching the hit areas on moves an element, or when a hit area is not an unscaled absolute box on its own element. A case compares the scan with a tap on every pixel for a hit area that overlaps a control and one that only touches it. At a load of 51 to 66, 20 runs per engine passed; with old and new files side by side at a load of 64 to 86, all three cases timed out in 10 of 10 old WebKit runs and in none of the new ones. The convention is in [Test in Chromium and WebKit](/en/docs/contributing/testing#test-in-chromium-and-webkit). No check, because the cost sits in helpers that a source rule cannot count per test.
