### Coalesced Mail hydration lost requests and repeatedly yielded its place

- **What happened:** New messages could remain without content until a later scheduled pass, and later bodies could finish first.
- **Root cause:** Coalescing merged a submission into a claimed job even after its final pending-body query. Completion released the key without preserving that request. Arrival-body hydration also had a 60-second priority disadvantage against envelope syncs and registered a new place after each yielded batch. Promoting every first-attempt batch was too broad: the existing fairness guards showed body backlogs displacing folder continuations and requested syncs. Only the first batch of a completed incremental arrival receives that promotion; history, body continuations and retries remain background work. Newest-first selection explains the observed completion order; it does not establish which delay affected each production message.
- **Error class:** Lost wake-ups at coalesced job completion and repeated priority inversion between stages of one user-visible operation.
- **Guard:** `hydration-scheduling.integration.test.ts` holds a claimed generation after its last body check, imports another message through folder sync, and requires completion without the minute scheduler, after both successful and failed batches. It also exercises four occupied workers, merged requests, persisted publication recovery, and operator timings with direct numeric assertions. It verifies that only the first arrival batch receives sync priority and later body batches remain background. `hydration-work.integration.test.ts` covers generation handoff, stale redelivery, transaction rollback, independent publication failure, and expired-delivery fencing. `provider-lease-fairness.integration.test.ts` gives hydration a one-second earlier place and proves that a later sync cannot use its former 60-second head start, while hydration still yields after one source. A later command ends a first-attempt batch and precedes its successor generation. Existing folder-continuation and requested-sync fairness assertions remain unchanged. The lifecycle operator projection guard checks exact timing values and excludes scheduling internals, while the platform-wide projection retains coverage counts. A lost job-heartbeat test requires the source transfer to abort before another body is claimed.
