### Failed shards must contribute runtime measurements

- **What happened:** WebKit shard 4 in run 40533200164 exhausted its unchanged 20-minute test budget during Notebooks' `full-width.browser.test.ts`. The same 33-suite composition finished in 10.7 minutes in run 40517212106. Every shard had a 13.4-minute prediction. Actual test execution times in shard order were 9.2, 12.8, 13.8, at least 20.0 (aborted), 13.2, 12.9, 13.0, 9.3, and 13.3 minutes. These exclude job setup and finalization.
- **Root cause:** An unexplained job-wide slowdown: shard 4's first 16 completed suites took 19.2 minutes against 5.1 predicted; their median measured-to-weight ratio was 3.9, compared with 0.7–1.0 in the other shards. The logs do not identify its hardware cause. Its Service Account desktop flow timed out at 30.1 seconds versus 7.9 seconds in the previous green run, also a factor of 3.8. That timeout cannot explain the roughly 14-minute excess across the completed suites. No independent Service Account failure is established by this log. Separately, the timing importer rejected all failed jobs, making these observations unavailable for subsequent planning.
- **Error class:** Job-wide runner variance exhausts a budget despite balanced historical file weights.
- **Guard:** `scripts/test-weights.test.ts` now imports completed suite timings from failed, timed-out, and cancelled jobs; preserves unexecuted suites; replaces old observations with the selected runs' maxima; and prunes deleted suites. The initial window included the original three runs and 40533200164, conservatively retaining the slow observations for this plan. They raised the old shard 4 composition to a 27.5-minute estimate. Before the Mail split, the workflow-matrix test failed at nine shards (904 seconds after rebalancing) and passed at ten (about 13.6 minutes each). After merging #1990, refreshing the browser window with run 40561248493 measures all 287 browser suites, removes the deleted Mail monolith, and puts the five Mail shell files on five separate shards. The updated plan needs 13.7 minutes at ten shards versus 15.2 at nine. The tenth shard adds headroom rather than proving that the same files will be slow again. Later refreshes can lower those weights. No budget, timeout, or runner changed. Static file weights cannot guard against an arbitrary slowdown of an entire future runner; interrupted suites still need a complete observation. [Issue #1998](https://github.com/k2b-dev/cloud/issues/1998) tracks the unexplained whole-job variance.
